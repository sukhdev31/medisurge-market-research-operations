# Management decision memo | synthetic exercise

**Question.** Which hospital segments, geographies and services should Medisurge investigate first, and how can research stay consistent and evidence grounded?

**What the public model shows.** A synthetic 180-account list can be sorted using six transparent criteria. Decision owners can change all weights and see ranking changes. For every account, unknown factors create a score interval, and unverified real records are routed to research. A hypothetical market model exposes service applicability, geographic capacity, capture and project value rather than displaying an unsupported market headline.

**Proposed operating decision.** Ask Medisurge's owner to confirm which of CSSD consultancy, NABH readiness and modular OT are active priorities, plus feasible geography. Verify 25 actual accounts using official sources, review the first ten scores with the owner, and only then use the score for research allocation. One person should own account research and one person should approve outreach. An expansion signal is an invitation to verify, never evidence of a planned purchase.

**30 days.** Conduct stakeholder interview; confirm data permissions and serviceability; populate 25 verified accounts with claim-level source IDs and checked dates. Measure manual research preparation time on five accounts.

**60 days.** Evaluate weight stability and source aging, obtain feedback, revise the scoring rubric and test an optional source constrained AI brief against a frozen set.

**90 days.** Pilot private deployment with access control and audit logging, compare verified account research time, and decide whether the expected benefit justifies ongoing data maintenance.

**Risks.** Public demo is simulated. The 180 rows do not estimate real hospital demand. Market scenario value and annual capacity are hypotheses. No current stakeholder review, verified competitor universe, observed savings, lead conversion or earned revenue is available.
